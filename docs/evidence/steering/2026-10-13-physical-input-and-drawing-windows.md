## A trial ends without a picture

Discovery and facing trials now lease their exact delivered key receipt.
A deadline releases that press even while interface work or observation
awaits a result. Input changes cancel the old lease. Rechoosing the same
press cannot extend it, and a late lease cannot release another receipt.

A current discovery release begins its planned finite neutral observation
phase. A preplanned counter pulse advances into its rest once, without
issuing another key. Changed identity still permits the physical release
but withholds the old experiment's rest measurement. An identity change
during release acknowledgement receives the same treatment.

Leaving a play owner cancels its observation tasks and deadlines. Its
primary release goes through the canonical delivery boundary and records
the actual neutral receipt. Later input windows do not inherit a physically
released key's old receipt.

## Drawing time and arrival time

Stream startup correlates supported browser clocks through bracketed CDP
requests. Frame swap metadata is mapped with a fixed clock offset for the
stream epoch. The browser's monotonic swap clock is preferred where it is
available. A wall-clock mapping is the fallback. Integer milliseconds in
the wall-clock read enlarge its capture uncertainty by one millisecond.

Queued frame messages keep the cadence of their drawing timestamps. Each
compatible float receipt carries capture bounds, arrival, source timestamp
and epoch. Those fields survive the private vision-worker boundary. A
stream cannot change clock domains midway through a measured path.
Repeated, regressed, unmapped or implausibly future source times cannot
renew a frame; protocol messages are still acknowledged.

Control attribution accepts a bounded capture only if its entire interval
belongs to one delivered input. Missing source metadata retains explicit
arrival semantics with no measured lower bound; it cannot establish
capture ownership. Scalar age estimates use the conservative capture lower
bound. Canvas and screenshot request timing is unchanged.

The clock correlation assumes equal clock rates over a short stream.
Wall-clock steps hidden within a delivery backlog are not fully detectable
without another correlation or monotonic source. Drift estimation and
periodic correlation are not implemented by this checkpoint.
